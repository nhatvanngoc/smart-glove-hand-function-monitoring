## Observed strengths to preserve

- Clear numbered sections and descriptive subheadings; readers can scan the document.
- A direct link from a practical problem to the proposed design and its evaluation.
- Comfort with specific technical terms and measurable criteria.
- Concise tables/lists where readers need to compare objectives, hardware or expected outputs.
- A genuine team rationale (“nhóm nghiên cứu lựa chọn…”) instead of hiding agency behind impersonal prose.

## Editing tendencies to watch

These are editing risks visible in the samples, not judgments about the author:

- Broad scene-setting openings can delay the actual research gap.
- Repeated summaries or “từ vấn đề đó…” transitions can restate a point without adding evidence.
- Highly specific thresholds, performance targets and technology names can sound like established capability unless explicitly marked as targets.
- Medical, neuroscience and causal language can outrun what a school engineering prototype measures.
- Citations and numbered bibliography entries need a line-by-line match; a plausible reference near a sentence is not automatically support for it.

## Practical target voice

Formal but plain Vietnamese. State the local problem or exact evidence early; use technical precision without promotional language; explain the design in concrete verbs; make the student/team's actual contribution visible; finish with what will be tested and what remains unknown. Vary sentence rhythm because the reasoning requires it, not to satisfy an AI detector.

## Non-goals

- Do not copy the agriculture/robotics sample's subject matter, references, sentence-level text, or broad claims.
- Do not imitate errors, inflate the student's voice, fabricate first-person experience, add intentional imperfections, or promise evasion of AI-detection systems.
- Do not “humanize” a claim by hiding that it is uncertain. Human-sounding prose must remain more, not less, accountable to evidence.
